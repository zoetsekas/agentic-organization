Getting started

        
The Agentic Designer is where an organisation of AI agents is
          designed, checked and published. You describe the organisation — its
          teams, agents, what they may do and with what data — as a
          model; the designer checks every change against the
          model's rules, and publishing turns the model into running systems.

The model, in five ideas

        
        
UML underneath
          The model is written in UML: every relationship is a
          composition (a part moves with its whole), an
          association (a reference, drawable from either end), a
          usage, a realisation or a deployment. That
          decides how it is drawn and what dragging does.

        

          
1 Organisation and teams

            
Every design has one organisation; it is the root team. Teams
              contain sub-teams and agents. An agent belongs to exactly one team.

          
2 Agents and sub-agents

            
An agent is a member of the organisation, with a team, a mandate
              and human counterparts. A sub-agent is a worker owned by one agent,
              called like a tool, and never wider than its parent.

          
3 What an agent may do

            
Capabilities (directly, or through roles — its own or its
              team's), knowledge it consults, skills, plugins and tools it holds,
              data it produces or relies on.

          
4 Where it runs

            
Each agent is deployed into one or more environments (sandboxes).

          
5 Rules

            
Policies, guardrails, separations of duties, decisions and
              mandates bound what anyone may decide.

Workflows: two levels

        
The seam is the
          interface A step built in an engine is governed at its
          edges: what it is given, what it calls and which data it touches.
          The engine's own graph never enters the design.

        
A workflow has two levels. The governed
          workflow is drawn here. What happens inside one of
          its steps may be built in an engine such as Langflow, LangGraph or
          your own code.

        
        

          
On the process canvasIt means

          
            
Agent step Tool step
              Work an agent or a tool does. The box says who owns it.

            
Human approval
              The workflow pauses for the named person or role. A human
              step with nobody named is an issue; in production it blocks.

            
BranchChooses one way out.

            
Fork … Join
              Every way out of the fork runs; the join waits for all of
              them. A fork whose branches never meet, or a join no fork leads
              to, is an issue.

            
External step (dashed)
              Calls a workflow whose body is external. It shows
              the engine from the binding the design was saved with, and
              Open in <engine> ↗ when the binding
              gives the flow's address.

          
        

        
    
          
  1. Add a workflow in Components → Workflows, set
            body to external and fill in its
            interface: inputs, outputs, the capabilities
            and endpoints it calls, and the data classes it receives and returns.
    2. 
          
  2. In the governed workflow, add a workflow step that calls
            it and set its owner. The owner must hold
            every capability the interface names and the data it is sent.
    3. 
          
  3. Bind the workflow to its engine in the target's binding
            (workflows: — engine, flow, and editor_url for
            the link). Without a binding, publishing is refused.
    4. 
        

        
Governed at its edges
          What an external step does inside is as safe as its declared interface
          is true. Calling an out-of-process engine is egress: the owner's
          allowlist, data classes and credentials are checked on every call, and
          the reply is treated as data. If the design has no saved binding the
          canvas cannot know the engine, and says so on the step.

        


        

        
Properties and the Components editors

        
Select a box to edit it in Properties (right). The
          Components menu opens the same form full-size for every kind
          — a list of them, the form, and what refers to each; Agents
          has its own screen.

        
    
          
  • Fields marked * are required.
    • 
          
  • References are searchable pickers: type to filter what the design
            has, choose to add, × to remove. If there is nothing to
            choose, the picker says what to add first.
    • 
          
  • A value the model refuses is put back, with the reason under the
            field.
    • 
        

        


        

        
Forms

        
Every form marks what it requires with *.
          On submit, each field that is missing or wrong is outlined and the
          reason is written under it; editing the field clears it.

        


        

        
Panels

        
Every side panel can be resized from its inner edge (double-click to
          reset), minimised –, maximised □ and restored

Reviewing authority

        
Declared vs granted
          A capability declared on a team is inherited by every agent in it;
          the review shows the combined result, not the individual
          declarations.

        
The canvas and forms show what was declared. The authority
          review shows what the design grants, resolved:

        
    
          
  • what each agent may actually decide once team inheritance, roles
            and mandates combine;
    • 
          
  • which pairs of decisions are kept apart and whether anyone holds
            both;
    • 
          
  • where each agent's work lives (its placement, a unit crossed with
            an environment);
    • 
          
  • and what each person is accountable for.
    • 
        

        
It is read-only — change the design to change it.

        
    
          
  • It is the first step of Publish…:
            nothing is requested until you tick that you have reviewed it.
    • 
          
  • Review authority… on the Org chart opens it any
            time; ← Back to the design returns you.
    • 
          
  • An agent's Effective authority — its resolved
            decisions, each marked declared here or inherited — is shown in its
            Properties and on the Agents screen, as of the last save.
    • 
        

        


        

        
Publishing

        
    
          
  1. Review what the design grants (above) and tick that you have.
    2. 
          
  2. The design is validated and compiled for a target:
            local LangGraph
            ADK MAF
            Terraform for AWS/Azure/GCP.
    3. 
          
  3. Request deployment asks the platform to run it.
            It is enabled once the design compiles, you may publish, and the
            review is ticked.
    4. 
        

        
Compilation is traced: every element and link of the model reaches
          what is built, or is named as not built.

        


        

        
Catalog and Marketplace

        
Catalog holds the approved building blocks a design
          may choose from — models, MCP servers, environment templates,
          permission sets, plugins, guardrails. A new entry is
          proposed until approved. Marketplace shares
          agents, skills, plugins, workflows, sandbox templates and sessions
          between organisations.

        


        

        
Sessions and Operations

        
Sessions lists runs of published organisations, each
          with its trace. Operations shows alerts and per-agent
          health.

Who may decide what

        
Effective authority, resolved the way the phase gate
          resolves it. A line reading inherited was never declared on
          the agent — it came down the tree.

        

          
Open an organisation to resolve its authority.

        

      

      

        
Separation of duties

        
A team is a scope and may hold both sides; an agent is
          a principal and may not.

        

        
Where the work lives

        
Placements: an org unit crossed with an environment
          class. Agents inside one share a volume and a process namespace and
          reach each other without a rule; everything not listed as reachable
          is denied.

        

        
People

        
What a person may decide. Nothing here says
          what they may reach: a person signs into the systems under their own
          employer's identity, and a permission this platform cannot enforce
          would be worse than none.
